When San Diego Demand Peaks, From Summer Beaches to Comic-Con

July and August are San Diego’s busiest visitor months.

Summer beach trips and Comic-Con push your occupancy and nightly rates to their highest of the year.

We load premium rates before summer bookings open, then adjust them to booking pace, because early bookers snap up any peak night left at an ordinary rate, below what it would earn later.

Comic-Con is one of San Diego’s biggest booking events: when the city announced it would stay through 2030, it expected more than 61,000 hotel room nights to be booked over the convention.

Each year we confirm the dates, price those nights apart from the rest of July and set minimum stays to match the convention’s length. Once hotels fill, attendees turn to rentals; general summer pricing undervalues those nights, and short stays break up your most valuable stretch.

San Diego Rental Licenses, Zoning and Occupancy Taxes

As of November 2026, these are the requirements to sort out before you start taking bookings in San Diego; the linked City Treasurer pages hold the official rules.

  • Before accepting bookings

    Before your first booking, confirm your rental license, your Business Tax Certificate if one applies to you, and your Transient Occupancy Tax registration for the correct tax zone.

  • Non-owner host obligations

    If you host a short-term rental in a home you don't own, you need a Business Tax Certificate, and you pay Business Tax every year.

  • Taxable stay length

    In the City of San Diego, short-term rentals of less than one month are subject to Transient Occupancy Tax.

  • Zone-specific tax rates

    Since May 1, 2025, Transient Occupancy Tax has been 11.75%, 12.75% or 13.75%, depending on which tax zone your property is in.

  • Unsettled compliance risks

    Taking bookings before your compliance is settled exposes you to penalties and cancelled reservations, so these checks come before any reservations do.

Frequently Asked Questions

If I sell my San Diego home, does my short-term rental license go to the buyer, or does the new owner have to apply again?

Your license doesn’t pass to the buyer; the new owner has to apply for their own. As of November 2026, San Diego short-term rental licenses can’t be transferred between owners or between properties. When a buyer’s purchase depends on short-term rental income, we advise them to plan their own licensing path from the start instead of relying on the seller’s license.

Could I rent to traveling nurses or Navy families for 30 days or more instead of nightly guests, and would the same city rules apply?

A stay of a full month or longer doesn’t need a San Diego STRO license. As of November 2026, the STRO licensing rules cover stays of under one month. We treat stays of a full month or longer as a separate option with different licensing requirements, and we go by the full-month threshold rather than counting every 30-day stay as the same thing.
