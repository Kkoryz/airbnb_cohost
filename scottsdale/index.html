Peak Seasons and Event-Driven Demand in Scottsdale

Scottsdale's February–March peak books out early, and so do the major event weeks.

We open your calendar early for these periods, with higher rates and longer minimum stays. The events that drive demand include the WM Phoenix Open, the Barrett-Jackson Collector Car Auction, the Scottsdale Arabian Horse Show and San Francisco Giants Spring Training.

In November–December, average daily rates ran about 30% lower in Experience Scottsdale's winter 2020 release, so we bring your rates down and loosen minimum stays for those softer late-year months. Bookings fill the calendar only when stays are easy to book and competitively priced.

Keeping peak-season rates and stay requirements through the slower period works against both of those.

Scottsdale Rental Licenses, Taxes and Local Rules

Here's what Scottsdale licensing, taxes and state law require as of November 2026; the linked city and state pages hold the official rules.

  • Annual city license

    For each property you rent for less than 30 days, you get and keep up an annual city license, which costs $250 per property.

  • Before the first booking

    You have the city short-term rental license in hand before you accept your first booking, rather than leaving it until guests arrive.

  • Additional government requirements

    The city license comes on top of county and state requirements; it doesn't replace any of those obligations for your property.

  • Local rental taxes

    On stays of 29 days or less, Scottsdale charges a 5.0% transient lodging tax on top of its 1.7% privilege tax on real property rentals.

  • Collection and filing

    You check which lodging and privilege taxes the platform collects and which ones you file yourself; any city, county or state taxes left unpaid remain your liability.

  • Limits on city regulation

    Arizona law bars cities, Scottsdale included, from restricting or regulating short-term rentals based on use or occupancy, apart from the specific ways the statute lists.

Challenges of Running a Rental in Scottsdale

In Scottsdale, back-to-back peak turnovers and desert heat make keeping the pool and air conditioning in shape essential to running your rental.

We tie servicing to your booking calendar and fit it into the gaps between stays, so consecutive turnovers never push maintenance back. When the AC or pool fails during a stay, refunds and bad reviews follow quickly.

After every stay, we arrange a clean with a local partner, and we adjust your rates against nearby listings, since this market also competes with luxury resorts.

To respect neighborhood noise limits, we set quiet hours from 10 pm to 7 am in your house rules and guest messages.

For your Scottsdale home, we arrange and coordinate the local partner companies for cleaning, key handover and property maintenance. Pool and air-conditioning servicing is scheduled into the gaps between stays, so back-to-back turnovers don't hold that work up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do summer air conditioning and pool costs in Scottsdale take out of my off-season rental income?

It comes down to your pool's size, how hard your air conditioning runs and your utility rates. Scottsdale's tiered water rates charge more per unit the more water you use, and the city's water conservation guidance puts a 400 sq. ft. pool's evaporation at about 19,665 gallons a year, mostly in summer. Send us your recent water and power bills, and we'll go through them against your off-season bookings on your discovery call.

What do monsoon storms and dust storms do to a rental that sits empty between bookings, and how should it be prepared?

Monsoon rains bring flash flooding to your rental. Maricopa County's monsoon guidance says sandbags filled 1/2 to 2/3 full and stacked in a brick pattern at doors and low entrances give it temporary protection.

Do most Scottsdale HOAs and gated communities allow nightly rentals, or do they set minimum stays I'd have to follow?

Your own Scottsdale HOA's rules decide whether your home allows nightly rentals and which rental restrictions you have to follow. As of November 2026, state law doesn't stop HOAs from regulating or restricting short-term rentals. If you read those rules before you buy or list, you know which rental strategy is open to your home. Where short stays are restricted, stays of 30 days or longer are an alternative only if the HOA's rules allow them.

Would I do better renting to snowbirds by the month in winter than taking short nightly stays?

With month-long stays, you give up the higher nightly rates of peak and event weeks in exchange for fewer turnovers and no city short-term rental license. Your HOA's rules decide which strategy you can use. As of November 2026, Scottsdale rentals of 30 days or longer don't need the city's short-term rental license. Stays of less than 30 days do need it, and the license costs $250 per property each year.
