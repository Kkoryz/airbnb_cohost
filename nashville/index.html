Nashville Permits, Taxes and Rental Rules

As of November 2026, these are Nashville's requirements; the linked Metro Nashville pages hold the official rules:

  • Operating permit

    You need a Metro Codes operating permit for your short-term rental. It is separate from the business licensing that depends on your income.

  • Under $3,000

    If your rental's annual gross income is under $3,000, you don't need a business tax license.

  • $3,000 to $99,999

    At this level of annual gross income, you need a Minimal Activity license, which costs $15/$30 a year.

  • $100,000 or more

    At this level, you need a Business Tax License with a one-time $15/$30 fee, and you file TDOR returns and pay the taxes.

  • Tax responsibility

    As the permit holder, you remit business, sales and hotel occupancy taxes to the city and state. That includes any occupancy and sales taxes the platforms don't collect or remit.

  • Stay limits

    These rules put no yearly cap on rental days. Each stay lasts at least twenty-four hours and no more than thirty consecutive days.

  • Income checks

    We advise you to confirm your license tier before you take bookings and to reassess it as your revenue grows. The licensing duties stay with you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run a short-term rental in a Nashville home I rent instead of own, and is my landlord's written permission enough?

A 2017 Metro Nashville audit of the permit program says you can apply as a lessee for a multifamily (Type III) short-term rental permit, for example for an apartment or condominium, and that your application must include signed documentation showing the owner's permission. Confirm the current rule with Metro Codes before you apply.

If my permit lapses or I sell the house, can a non-owner-occupied permit be issued again for that address in my zone?

As of November 2026, no new non-owner-occupied short-term rental permits are issued in AR2A, R, RS or RM zones. You can renew an existing permit in those zones, but it ends when the property is sold or transferred, and it doesn't carry over to the buyer. Check the property's zoning and permit status before you buy.

Does a typical homeowner's policy in Nashville cover tornado or flood damage while guests are staying there?

Not reliably. Tennessee's insurance regulator noted in December 2023 that most standard homeowners' policies cover storm damage from wind, wind-driven rain, trees or other falling objects. Whether flood damage is covered, and whether any cover applies while paying guests stay, depends on your own policy. Before go-live, we ask for a policy that explicitly covers short-term rental use with paying guests, so ask your insurer to confirm in writing that it covers tornado and flood damage during a stay.
